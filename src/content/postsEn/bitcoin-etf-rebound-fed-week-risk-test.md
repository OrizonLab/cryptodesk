U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs finished last week with a headline number that looked constructive: $301.2 million of net inflows. The more important detail came at the end. Investors pulled $773.5 million from the products on Thursday and Friday, giving back most of the $1.05 billion that arrived earlier in the week.

That reversal leaves Bitcoin facing a difficult test as the Federal Open Market Committee begins its October 27–28 meeting. The question for American market participants is not simply whether ETF demand is positive. It is whether that demand can survive a risk-off session, a hawkish Federal Reserve message, or another move below $80,000.

## The weekly inflow hides a sharp reversal

Data published by Farside Investors shows that U.S.-listed spot Bitcoin funds attracted $301.2 million during the week ended October 23. That ended a two-week streak of weekly outflows: the funds lost $228.4 million the previous week and about $680 million the week before that, according to the same series.

The path was uneven. Monday through Wednesday produced approximately $1.05 billion of combined inflows. BlackRock’s IBIT accounted for $565.7 million of the week’s total inflows, while Fidelity’s FBTC and ARK 21Shares’ ARKB recorded net weekly outflows of $116.6 million and $99.6 million respectively.

Then the direction changed. The funds recorded about $635.6 million of outflows on October 22 and another $137.9 million on October 23. IBIT lost $110.3 million on Friday and FBTC lost $31.8 million. The two-day exit represented roughly three quarters of the money that had entered the funds earlier in the week.

This is not the same as saying the ETF structure has failed. The products still have substantial cumulative net inflows, and a single week can be distorted by rebalancing, profit-taking, or changes in short-term futures positioning. But the sequence matters: demand appeared when Bitcoin was moving higher and weakened quickly when the price retreated.

## Bitcoin is meeting a tougher macro backdrop

Bitcoin was trading near $80,000 on October 26 after slipping below that level during the weekend and again during Monday trading. The market is looking toward Washington because the Fed’s next decision arrives after a rate increase in September.

The Federal Reserve’s official calendar lists a two-day FOMC meeting on October 27–28, with the decision scheduled for 2:00 p.m. Eastern Time on October 28 and a press conference at 2:30 p.m. The committee raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points in September, to 3.75%–4%, while saying inflation remained elevated.

Market expectations reported by financial data providers point to a high probability of no change this week. That does not remove the risk for crypto. The communication around the decision may matter more than the mechanical rate decision, particularly if officials signal that another increase in December remains possible.

The macro backdrop is already uncomfortable. Oil prices have been elevated by geopolitical risk, while technology stocks have been pressured by renewed concern about the cost and financing of artificial-intelligence investment. Bitcoin has traded as a high-beta risk asset during similar episodes: when investors reduce exposure to equities and leverage, crypto liquidity can thin quickly.

## The on-chain picture is not a clean confirmation

CryptoKeyhole’s weekly report for October 19–25 estimated net deposits of 10,192 BTC to the exchanges it tracks, lifting the covered balance to about 1.13 million BTC. It also counted 1,602 transfers of at least 100 BTC during the week, up 27.2% from the previous week.

Those figures should not be treated as a direct sell signal. Exchange wallets are incomplete, and large transfers can reflect custody operations, internal movements, or collateral management rather than an intention to sell. Still, net deposits alongside weak price action are relevant to a market already watching ETF redemptions.

The same report estimated that stablecoin supply on Ethereum declined by $705.3 million over the week, to $163.3 billion. A smaller stablecoin base can mean redemptions exceeded issuance, although it does not measure all dollar liquidity available to crypto markets. Together, the figures describe a market with less obvious marginal liquidity, not a confirmed directional forecast.

## What U.S. investors should watch next

The first signal is the ETF tape after the Fed announcement. If inflows return despite a firm central-bank message, the market may be showing that institutional demand can absorb macro pressure. If outflows accelerate, the late-week reversal will look less like temporary profit-taking and more like a risk-management response.

The second signal is price behavior around $80,000 and the nearby options market. Market coverage identified the October 28 $77,000 put and $84,000 call among the busiest contracts, with puts still carrying a premium in one-month volatility pricing. That positioning does not predict the outcome, but it shows where traders are concentrating protection and upside exposure.

The third signal is whether Bitcoin can reclaim the $82,500 area that it lost late last week. A recovery above that level would not settle the macro question, but it would show buyers are willing to defend the dip. Conversely, a sustained move below $80,000 would put more focus on the approximately $77,000 area cited by market analysts as the next major technical reference.

The core message is simple: the $301 million weekly ETF inflow is real, but it is not the whole story. The timing of the withdrawals, the upcoming Fed communication, and the signs of thinner liquidity make this week a test of demand rather than a victory lap for the Bitcoin ETF trade.
