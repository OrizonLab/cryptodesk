Bitcoin is heading into a week with fewer Federal Reserve headlines and a more important question for the market: will buyers return when the central bank stops talking? The Fed’s pre-meeting quiet period began on October 17, according to its calendar, leaving Treasury yields, oil and the next round of market positioning to carry the rate story into the October 27–28 meeting.

That silence arrives after a difficult data sequence. September consumer prices rose 0.6% in the month and 3.7% over the year, while producer prices jumped 1.3% in September, the biggest monthly increase since March 2022. At the same time, US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately $228.4 million in net outflows over the five trading sessions through October 16. Bitcoin recovered after briefly trading below $80,000, but the rebound has not yet demonstrated that institutional demand has returned.

For US readers, the tension is straightforward: the market has reduced the immediate probability of another rate hike, but inflation and energy costs still make an easy-money narrative difficult to defend.

## The Fed can no longer guide the market in public

The Federal Reserve’s official calendar lists the next FOMC meeting for October 27 and 28, with the decision and press conference scheduled for October 28. Between the start of the quiet period and that decision, officials generally do not provide fresh public guidance on monetary policy. That removes one of the fastest ways markets update their expectations.

The previous meeting remains a significant reference point. The minutes of the September 15–16 meeting say participants supported a 25-basis-point increase, taking the federal funds target range to 3.75%–4%. The minutes also state that most participants viewed another increase by year-end as likely appropriate, while emphasizing that future decisions would depend on incoming data.

That combination matters for Bitcoin. The policy rate is already restrictive by the Fed’s own recent framing, and the September minutes did not close the door on another hike. But with officials temporarily quiet, traders must infer the path from inflation releases, oil prices, bond yields and risk appetite rather than from speeches.

## Inflation made the next decision less comfortable

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that September’s CPI rose 0.6% on a seasonally adjusted basis. The annual rate accelerated to 3.7%, from 3.4% in August. Core CPI increased 0.3% in the month and 2.6% over 12 months. Gasoline rose 9.1% in September and accounted for more than half of the monthly all-items increase; the energy index rose 5.2%.

The PPI report delivered another warning. Final-demand producer prices increased 1.3% in September, after rising 0.5% in August. The annual increase reached 6.3%, and final-demand goods prices rose 2.6%. More than 80% of the monthly increase in final-demand goods was attributed to energy, according to the BLS release.

Neither report dictates what the Fed will do. They do, however, make the distinction between a temporary energy shock and persistent inflation especially important. A central bank trying to bring inflation back toward 2% cannot easily promise rapid easing while headline prices are accelerating.

Bitcoin is sensitive to that debate because it does not pay a conventional yield. When Treasury yields rise, the opportunity cost of holding a volatile, non-income-producing asset also rises. This is not a complete model of Bitcoin’s price, but it helps explain why rate expectations can move crypto even when the underlying network has not changed.

## ETF flows show that the marginal buyer is cautious

Farside Investors’ daily table shows a volatile rather than one-directional ETF market. US spot Bitcoin funds attracted $175.7 million on October 13, then lost $261.4 million on October 14. The market recorded a small $5.1 million inflow on October 15, followed by an $82.4 million outflow on October 16. Across the five sessions from October 12 through 16, the net result was approximately negative $228.4 million.

The composition is also useful. BlackRock’s IBIT and Fidelity’s FBTC accounted for most of the October 16 withdrawal, with IBIT down $71.8 million and FBTC down $10.6 million in the tracker’s latest breakdown. The flows do not identify investor motives. A redemption can reflect a bearish view, portfolio rebalancing, tax planning or the closing of a short-term trade.

The correct conclusion is therefore limited: the ETF channel was not supplying steady marginal demand during a week of macro stress. It is too early to conclude that US institutions have abandoned Bitcoin. The next signal would be several consecutive sessions of creations, ideally spread across more than one major fund.

## Prediction markets see a rebound, not certainty

The supplied Polymarket snapshot shows a market that has become more constructive over the last 24 hours. The probability of Bitcoin trading above $80,000 on October 21 rose from 70.5% to 76.0%, while the October 20 contract moved from 75.5% to 80.0%. The October 19 contract for above $80,000 rose from 82.5% to 86.5%.

Those numbers are indicative probabilities from a prediction market, not a forecast or a fundamental valuation. They describe the prices participants are willing to trade, and liquidity is uneven across contracts. The move is still informative as a measure of short-term positioning: traders are pricing a recovery above $80,000 as more likely than they did a day earlier, even while ETF flows remain soft.

That divergence is the story. Price can stabilize before long-term capital returns, especially when short sellers cover or derivatives positioning resets. A durable recovery would require confirmation from spot ETF creations, healthier risk appetite and a bond market that is no longer pushing yields higher.

## Three signals will decide whether the rebound has depth

First, watch daily ETF flows. One positive session would be encouraging but inconclusive; a sequence of inflows across IBIT, FBTC and smaller funds would be stronger evidence that allocators are rebuilding exposure.

Second, watch the 10-year Treasury yield and energy prices. The September inflation reports showed how quickly energy can dominate the headline. If oil pushes inflation expectations and yields higher, Bitcoin may continue to trade as a high-beta risk asset even without new crypto-specific bad news.

Third, watch what happens when the Fed speaks again. The October 28 statement and press conference will matter less for the fact of a decision than for the language around another year-end move. A clear hold signal could remove one source of pressure. A renewed emphasis on upside inflation risks could keep the buyer waiting.

The coming week is therefore not a simple bullish-or-bearish referendum. Bitcoin has recovered from its sub-$80,000 test, and short-term market pricing has improved. But the institutional demand channel is still being tested, while the Fed is silent and inflation remains uncomfortably high. The market now has to prove that the rebound can survive without a fresh wave of ETF buying.
