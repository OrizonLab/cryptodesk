The US market is about to get a more aggressive bitcoin wrapper, but the headline approval is easier to misunderstand than it looks.

On October 2, the Securities and Exchange Commission approved Cboe BZX Exchange’s proposed rule change to list and trade a 3x Bitcoin ETF, one of six triple-leveraged products covering bitcoin, ether, gold, silver, crude oil and natural gas. The order, Release No. 34-106577, is an official change to the exchange’s listing framework. It is not a promise that the fund is already trading, and it is not the same thing as approval for a spot bitcoin fund with three times the daily movement.

That distinction matters as bitcoin sits near $80,000 and US investors head into a busy week for the Federal Reserve. A new leveraged product can expand access to short-term exposure precisely when volatility, rates and liquidity are already moving together.

## What the SEC actually approved

The SEC approved Cboe BZX’s rule filing under Section 19(b)(2) of the Securities Exchange Act. The products are series of the VS Trust, sponsored by Volatility Shares. The proposed 3x Bitcoin ETF is designed to seek daily results equal to three times the daily performance of a benchmark built from bitcoin futures.

That structure answers an important question: the product does not plan to hold spot bitcoin as its core exposure. It expects to use futures contracts, cash and cash equivalents serving as collateral or margin. If futures become unavailable or position limits become binding, the filing describes additional tools, including later-dated futures, benchmark-linked exchange-traded products and options.

The SEC’s order says the products meet the relevant requirements for commodity-based trust shares except for the multiple-return feature. It also relies on the exchange’s surveillance arrangements, the availability of pricing information and the fund website’s disclosure obligations. In plain English, the decision is primarily a listing approval. It gives the exchange a regulatory route to list the shares; it does not erase the operational steps required before investors can trade them.

## Approval is not the launch bell

The registration statement still has to become effective before the fund can begin operating. The SEC order does not set a launch date. That means investors should not read the October 2 decision as confirmation that a ticker is live, liquid or available through every US brokerage account.

This is a recurring source of confusion in crypto markets. A rule-change approval can establish that an exchange may list a product under its rules. A fund’s registration statement, seed capital, market-making arrangements and final exchange notice determine whether the product actually reaches the market. Those steps can be quick, but they are separate from the Commission’s order.

The distinction also prevents an exaggerated comparison with the spot bitcoin ETFs approved in January 2024. Spot funds hold bitcoin or have direct exposure to its price. The newly approved product is built around futures and targets a daily multiple. Its behavior can therefore diverge materially from three times bitcoin’s return over a week, a month or a year.

## Why 3x daily exposure is a different instrument

A daily leveraged fund resets its target every session. Suppose bitcoin rises 10% one day and falls 9.09% the next. The underlying asset is roughly back where it started. A fund targeting three times the daily move would gain about 30% on the first day and lose roughly 27.27% on the second, leaving it below its starting value before fees, financing and tracking effects.

The example is simplified, but the mechanism is real. In a choppy market, compounding can erode returns even when the underlying asset finishes a period flat. In a strongly trending market, the effect can work in the opposite direction. Neither outcome is a simple promise of three times bitcoin’s move across a holding period.

Futures introduce another layer. The fund’s return can be affected by the futures curve, collateral income, roll costs, margin requirements and the difference between the benchmark and the spot market. During fast moves, these features can make the product’s intraday result differ from what a headline bitcoin price suggests.

The SEC’s own investor education materials have repeatedly warned that leveraged and inverse ETFs are designed around daily objectives. The order’s approval should therefore be read as a market-access decision, not as an endorsement that the structure is suitable for a particular investor.

## The timing raises the stakes

The decision arrives as bitcoin trades in a market already focused on leverage. Bitcoin briefly fell below $80,000 over the weekend, while more than $800 million in crypto positions were reported liquidated over a 24-hour period, with long positions making up most of the total. US spot bitcoin ETFs ended the latest trading week with approximately $301 million in net inflows, but that headline hides a sharp reversal: roughly $1.07 billion entered from Monday through Wednesday, followed by about $773.5 million in withdrawals on Thursday and Friday.

Those numbers do not prove that a leveraged ETF caused the move. The 3x product was not yet confirmed as trading, and ETF flows do not reveal the motive behind every redemption. They do show the environment into which a product like this would arrive: investors are watching leverage, weekend liquidity and the Federal Reserve’s next signal at the same time.

The Federal Open Market Committee meets October 27–28. A change in rate expectations can move the dollar, Treasury yields and the financing cost behind futures positions. A daily leveraged bitcoin fund would be exposed to that repricing through both the underlying market and its own derivatives mechanics.

## What US readers should watch next

First, watch for an effective registration statement and a formal launch notice. Until those appear, the product remains an approved listing route rather than a live trading opportunity.

Second, read the fund’s final prospectus rather than relying on the 3x label. The benchmark definition, fees, futures exposure, collateral policy, rebalance schedule and risk disclosures will determine how the product behaves. The words “three times” describe a daily objective, not a guaranteed long-term multiple.

Third, compare the product’s trading volume and spreads with its net asset value after launch. A new ETP can have limited liquidity at first. Wide spreads and volatile premiums or discounts can add costs that are invisible in the simple leverage headline.

Finally, separate access from demand. The approval expands the menu of instruments available to US traders, but it does not establish that institutions are buying bitcoin, that spot ETF flows will improve or that prices must rise. It is a plumbing development with market consequences, not a directional signal.

The SEC’s decision is material because it normalizes a more powerful way to package bitcoin exposure inside a US-listed product. The catch is that the product’s complexity rises with its leverage. For readers watching the next launch, the important question is not only whether 3x bitcoin can trade. It is whether the market understands what resets every day, what is held in futures and what can happen when volatility arrives before the fund has time to adjust.
