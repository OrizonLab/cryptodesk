The bitcoin demand signal that looked strong on Wednesday snapped back on Thursday. US spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds lost $635.6 million in net assets on October 22, according to data compiled by Farside Investors and reported by The Block. It was the largest daily outflow since June and more than erased the $528.4 million the funds added the day before.

The number matters because these ETFs have become one of the clearest institutional access points to bitcoin in the United States. But the timing matters just as much. The withdrawal arrived as technology stocks sold off, Treasury yields moved higher, oil climbed above $116 a barrel and the Federal Reserve prepared for its October 27–28 meeting. For US readers, the immediate question is not whether bitcoin has a long-term story. It is whether demand can hold when the macro backdrop stops helping.

## A one-day reversal after $1 billion of inflows

Thursday’s outflow followed three positive sessions. The funds added about $526.7 million on October 19, $23.2 million on October 20 and $528.4 million on October 21, according to The Block’s summary of daily ETF data. Together, that was roughly $1.08 billion of net buying before Thursday gave back more than half of it in a single session.

The selling was broad, but not evenly distributed. BlackRock’s IBIT led the withdrawals with $254.1 million. Fidelity’s FBTC lost $217.5 million and ARK Invest and 21Shares’ ARKB lost $109.4 million. Morgan Stanley’s MSBT added $2.3 million, while several other funds were flat or recorded outflows.

Different trackers can show slightly different month-to-date totals because of reporting times and revisions. The robust conclusion is narrower: October’s ETF recovery weakened sharply, and the latest daily session was the largest withdrawal in months. A single day is not a trend, but it is a meaningful stress test.

## Why bitcoin did not collapse with the flow

Bitcoin traded near $81,460 on Friday and was broadly flat over 24 hours in data cited by Decrypt. That relatively calm price reaction may look surprising beside a $635.6 million fund outflow. It is not necessarily a contradiction.

ETF flows measure net creations and redemptions, not every exchange trade. An investor can redeem shares to reduce risk, raise cash or rebalance a portfolio without the entire amount becoming an immediate market sell order. Other buyers can also absorb the supply through exchanges or derivatives. The market can therefore register a large ETF outflow while the spot price remains inside a narrow range.

The ETF products also have a much longer record than the latest session. Since their US launch in January 2024, cumulative net inflows have exceeded $57 billion, with assets above $100 billion according to figures cited by The Block. That history does not guarantee future demand, but it explains why one reversal should be treated as a signal to monitor rather than a final verdict.

The more useful question is persistence. If inflows return after the US market digests Thursday’s risk reduction, the episode may look like portfolio housekeeping. If withdrawals continue while bitcoin loses the $80,000–$81,000 area, the demand picture would be materially weaker.

## Macro turned less friendly at the same time

The ETF reversal was part of a broader risk-off session. CoinDesk reported that the S&P 500 was lower on Friday and that the Nasdaq had fallen 2.6% on Thursday. The US 10-year Treasury yield moved back toward 4.99%, while Brent crude traded above $116 a barrel as geopolitical tensions renewed inflation concerns.

That combination is uncomfortable for a liquidity-sensitive asset. Higher yields can increase the opportunity cost of holding volatile assets. A stronger oil price can make the inflation path harder for central bankers. Weak technology shares also matter because bitcoin has traded increasingly alongside high-beta growth exposure rather than behaving like a standalone hedge.

The Federal Reserve’s own recent communication points in the same direction. Governor Christopher Waller said in an October 8 speech that inflation remained too high, the labor market was stable and additional rate hikes could be needed if the data supported that view. The Fed’s calendar lists the next two-day FOMC meeting for October 27–28. That does not tell us what policymakers will do, but it sets a clear near-term event risk for US markets.

## The prediction-market signal is not a price target

The supplied Polymarket snapshot adds context, but not certainty. Contracts implying bitcoin above $80,000 on October 24 rose to 97.3%, while the probability of being above $82,000 fell to 10.5%. Those numbers describe a market pricing a narrow range around current levels; they do not establish a forecast or explain the ETF flows.

Prediction-market probabilities are indicative and can move with liquidity, positioning and headline risk. They should be read as a sentiment gauge, not as investment advice or a substitute for primary market data.

## What to watch next week

Three indicators should help separate a one-day shock from a broader change in demand.

First, watch the next US spot ETF flow reports. A return to inflows would show that Thursday’s redemptions did not become a sustained exit. Repeated outflows would carry more information than the headline number alone.

Second, watch the $80,000–$82,500 zone and the ETF cohort’s estimated average cost basis, reported by Glassnode at about $82,313 in CoinDesk’s Friday coverage. A price below that level is not automatically bearish, but it can make ETF holders more sensitive to further volatility.

Third, watch yields, oil and the Fed’s language together. A crypto-specific recovery is harder to sustain when real-world funding conditions are tightening. Conversely, stabilizing yields and softer energy prices could give risk assets room to recover even if Thursday’s flow remains a blemish.

The takeaway is a tension between two facts: more than $1 billion entered US spot bitcoin ETFs over three sessions, then $635.6 million left in one day. Bitcoin held near $81,000, but the macro backdrop became less forgiving. That is a demand test, not a directional verdict.
